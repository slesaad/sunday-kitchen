Growing the plan

Weeks 1–2: core system only, no projects. Week 3: the seitan session. Week 5: add tempeh if the Asian grocery stocks it.

The three seitan flavors aren't three new dinners — they rotate into slots that already exist. Char siu alternates with Monday's sticky tofu, chorizo swaps into Friday's tacos, and shawarma turns the Mediterranean lunch bowl into a shawarma bowl, taking it from ~33 g protein to ~50 g. No new shopping, no new sauces. A seitan week is less prep-day work, not more.

Two

Ten lunch containers

Assembled on the weekend, reheated at work, zero weekday cost. Pack Monday through Wednesday on Sunday; pack Thursday and Friday on Wednesday night, so nothing you eat is five days old.

Friday's ragù goes over rice, not pasta. Reheated pasta in a container turns gluey by Friday; rice takes exactly the same sauce and holds up for four days.

Anything crisp or creamy travels separately. Tahini dressed on Sunday turns grains to paste by Tuesday, and crumbles packed against wet rice lose everything that makes them worth making.

The Mediterranean bowls are good cold — no microwave needed.

The Mediterranean bowls use no crumbles at all. Their protein is the roasted chickpeas off the harissa tray plus tahini, which keeps chickpeas and crumbles from competing for the same slot.

1:40 — 2:25 · Both of you: cool, portion, label

Forty-five minutes, and it's the part everyone underestimates — you're filling roughly twenty containers.

  • Freeze half of both anchors in bags laid flat. They stack, and flat bags thaw in an hour instead of a day.
  • Rice: half fridge, half frozen flat. Frozen rice microwaves better than four-day-old fridge rice, and it skips the food-safety question entirely.
  • Pack Monday to Wednesday's lunches now. Thursday and Friday get packed Wednesday night.
  • Crumbles get their own container, zones kept apart.
  • Label with the date. You will not remember.

Week B — freezer week, ~50 min

Move the frozen anchors to the fridge Friday night. Then: crumbles into the oven at 0:00, one veg tray at 0:15 (different vegetables, different spice), rice into the Instant Pot at 0:25, stir-fry pack at 0:28, one new sauce at 0:35, air-fry the tofu at 0:40, portion at 0:50.

The anchors are already made, and they'll taste better than the week you cooked them.

Supplements

Two labels worth checking. Your tofu should say calcium sulfate — that's what makes it a serious calcium source now that dairy is gone. Your soy milk should be fortified with calcium, B12 and D; unfortified soy milk is just beans and water.

The numbers

Protein sits at the upper end of the usual range on purpose — plant proteins have lower leucine density and slightly lower digestibility, so aiming high closes the gap. Soy does the heavy lifting: tofu, edamame and soy milk are complete proteins with good leucine.

Portion at the plate, not in the pot

There's a 1.7× spread between you, so cooking two versions of everything would double the work. Cook one, and add a booster when serving.

½ cup edamame = 9 g · 1 cup = 18 g · 100 g tofu = 10 g · ¼ cup crumbles = 8 g · 3 tbsp hemp seeds = 10 g · 2 tbsp peanut butter = 8 g.

A note on Person A's calories

That target is deliberately not a deficit. At 100–106 lb, "gain muscle and lose fat" is best served by eating at maintenance or slightly above, with high protein and progressive overload.

Eating less at that bodyweight costs lean mass, which moves you away from the goal. The leanness comes from adding muscle, not subtracting food.

Four gaps that actually matter

B12 — no reliable plant source, non-negotiable. Iodine — sea salt has none and most multivitamins skip it; cook with iodized salt. Algal DHA/EPA — flax gives ALA, which converts at a few percent. Vitamin D — especially October to March.

Everything else takes care of itself on a diet this varied.

Iron, without a supplement

Plant iron absorbs poorly alone, but vitamin C multiplies absorption several times over. This plan does it by accident — lemon on the chana, tomatoes in the ragù, peppers in the roasted veg, lime on the tacos. Keep it deliberate.

Don't drink tea or coffee with meals. The polyphenols cut absorption substantially. Wait an hour.

Creatine

3–5 g daily, timing irrelevant, no loading phase. Keep taking it.

Creatine comes almost entirely from meat, so vegetarians start with lower muscle stores — and tend to see larger gains from supplementing than omnivores do.

What to actually track

Weigh your protein sources for one week only — after that you'll eyeball 150 g of tofu. Read the weekly average of your morning weight, not the daily number.

Track your lifts. Progressive overload causes the muscle gain; the food permits it. Re-evaluate after 6–8 weeks, and adjust rice and oats before you touch protein.

Ramp the fiber up over two weeks. This plan lands around 45–55 g a day. If you're currently eating half that, start with smaller legume portions and build. Drink more water than you think you need — otherwise the first week is unpleasant in an entirely avoidable way.

Your first prep session will run past 2:45 — you'll be reading while cooking, and you won't know where anything is yet. The second one lands on the clock. If it doesn't, take the trim list rather than rushing the onions.
