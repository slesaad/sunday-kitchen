# Nutrition

## Targets

| | Person A (~103 lb / 47 kg) | Person B (~183 lb / 83 kg) |
|---|---|---|
| **Protein** | 90–95 g/day | 155–165 g/day |
| **Calories** | 1,900–2,050 | 2,600–2,850 |
| **Fiber** | 30 g+ | 38 g+ |

Protein sits at the upper end of the usual 1.6–2.2 g/kg range on purpose. Plant proteins
have lower leucine density and slightly lower digestibility than animal proteins, so
aiming high closes the gap. Soy does most of the heavy lifting here — tofu, edamame, and
soy milk are complete proteins with good leucine content, which is why the plan leans on
them.

**Spread protein across 3–4 feedings of 25–40 g** rather than loading one meal. Muscle
protein synthesis responds to per-meal dose, not just the daily total. This plan does that
naturally: shake, lunch, dinner, and a snack if needed.

### One note on the calorie targets

**Person A is deliberately not in a deficit.** At 100–106 lb, "gain muscle and lose fat" is
best served by eating at maintenance or slightly above with high protein and progressive
overload in the gym. Eating less at that bodyweight costs lean mass, which moves you away
from the goal — the leanness comes from adding muscle, not from subtracting food.

**Person B is at roughly maintenance to a slight surplus.** If body composition isn't
moving after 6–8 weeks, adjust the *carbohydrates* — rice and oats — up or down by a
quarter cup. **Leave the protein alone.** It's the one number that shouldn't move.

## Micronutrients — what actually needs attention

Going plant-based has four real gaps and a couple of things worth managing. Everything
else takes care of itself on a diet this varied.

### The four that matter

**B12.** Not optional. There is no reliable plant source. 250 mcg daily, or 2,500 mcg once
a week — both work, the body regulates absorption. Check your multivitamin; many cover it
adequately, and if so you're done.

**Iodine.** Quietly the most-missed one. Sea salt and kosher salt contain essentially none,
and most multivitamins skip it. **Cook with iodized salt** and the problem disappears at
zero cost. Check your multi's label before buying anything.

**Omega-3 (DHA/EPA).** The flax in your shake provides ALA, but humans convert ALA to DHA
at only a few percent. Algal oil is the direct source — it's where fish get it from
anyway. 250–500 mg daily.

**Vitamin D.** Widely low regardless of diet. 1,000–2,000 IU daily, especially in winter.
Get the D3 from lichen rather than lanolin if you want it vegan.

### Worth managing, no supplement needed

**Iron.** Plant (non-heme) iron absorbs poorly on its own, but **vitamin C multiplies
absorption several times over.** This plan does that by accident — the lemon on the chana,
the peppers in the roasted veg, the lime on the tacos, the lemon on Tuesday's curry. Keep it
deliberate. **Don't drink tea or coffee with meals** — the polyphenols cut iron absorption
substantially. Wait an hour. If Person A menstruates, this matters more; worth checking
ferritin at your next physical.

**Calcium.** With dairy gone, your sources are calcium-set tofu (check the label for
*calcium sulfate* — most extra-firm is), fortified soy milk, tahini, and leafy greens.
Between the tofu and the soy milk this plan lands near 1,000 mg on a normal day. Fine as
long as both are the right kind — see the shopping list notes.

**Zinc.** Legumes, nuts, and seeds cover it. Soaking beans (which you're doing anyway)
reduces the phytates that block absorption.

## Fiber

This plan will put you around **45–55 g of fiber a day**, comfortably above target and
genuinely good for you. But if you're currently eating half that, **ramp up over two weeks
rather than jumping straight in** — start with smaller legume portions and increase. Drink
more water than you think you need. Otherwise the first week is unpleasant in an entirely
avoidable way.

## Creatine

Keep taking it. **3–5 g daily, timing irrelevant, no loading phase needed** — loading just
gets you to saturation a couple of weeks sooner.

Worth knowing: creatine comes almost entirely from meat, so vegetarians and vegans start
with lower muscle stores — and consequently tend to see **larger** gains from
supplementing than omnivores do. It's one of the few supplements with genuinely strong
evidence, and it's more useful for you than for most people.

## What to actually track

Don't weigh food forever. Do this instead:

1. **Weigh your protein sources for one week only.** Tofu, edamame, and powder. After a
   week you'll eyeball 150 g of tofu accurately and never need the scale again.
2. **Weigh yourself in the morning, same conditions, and look at the weekly average.** Daily
   readings are mostly water and noise.
3. **Track your lifts.** Progressive overload is the actual driver of muscle gain. The food
   is what permits it — the training is what causes it.
4. **Re-evaluate after 6–8 weeks**, not sooner. Body composition moves slowly enough that
   anything shorter is guesswork.
